# Astra proposal

## 1. Verdict in five sentences

The ops list is too housekeeping-led: the most valuable next build is an end-to-end delivery trace and recovery contract, showing why a particular worker outcome did or did not reach its owner. Independent in-memory probes reproduced delivery through an ambiguous selector, admission of an expired owner's token, and a Claude summary showing only two of twenty outcomes while the hook records all twenty for acknowledgement. Finish Group 1's inspection surfaces, but do not delay evaluation for garbage collection or sweep owner rows before settling the long-Claude-turn semantics. Drop the proposed automatic `typebox` dependency change and the claim that the version pin is unenforced; the two wake-policy strings are also identical in this checkout, not drifted. Claude's idle gap disqualifies this bridge as the sole unattended dispatch-loop wake mechanism, and the skills should become five short entrypoints over shared references rather than another universal orchestration manual.

**The architectural correction is consistency between stages, not replacing SQLite or introducing a message broker.** Existing obligation IDs, per-row token fencing and event-time snapshots are good foundations. Extend them into a trace: `eventId → subscription revision/resolution → obligationId → delivery attempt ID → harness witness → ack/retire/dead-letter reason`. Store transition metadata without tokens or full secret-bearing transcripts; keep unacked work, prune retained audit data by an explicit policy. This also makes a post-expiry takeover reason useful—changing the response word without retaining the transition does not create an audit trail.

### Is the owner/lease primitive right?

**Yes for one delivery consumer; no as the entire notion of “the lead.”** Keep possession-based fencing and one reactor per profile. Separate the intended lead/assignment from temporary delivery eligibility, and advertise `idle-wake`, `turn-boundary-only`, or `pull-only`. A live Pi heartbeat proves extension responsiveness, not that its model has authority to act; a quiet Claude is not necessarily abandoned.

Do not elect owners from cwd, steal on a rejection, or permit two harnesses to race automatic reactions. Prefer explicit release/claim handoff; add atomic transfer only if handoffs prove frequent. Pin a roster to discovered session + workspace + pane/session incarnation for a bounded task, allowing name-following only when explicitly intended. Names can be reused and panes can move; show drift and require rebind instead of silently broadening a selector. Do not replace selectors with opaque IDs everywhere: their human-readable intent is useful once cardinality and incarnation are enforced.

The trust boundary also needs an honest sentence. The owner token protects lease consumption, but `profile.subscribe`, `inbox.retire` and `operation.dispatch` are separate mutation surfaces without that ownership check in `ObservabilityRpcServer`. Same-user agents are not OS-isolated from each other. Either explicitly trust local CLI clients and restrict their tool authority in packets, or add scoped management capabilities if untrusted workers must reach the socket. Do not claim lease-token secrecy makes every daemon mutation owner-authorized.

### “Not worth fixing” — acceptable limits versus product failures

- **Idle Claude delivery: acceptable adapter limitation, unacceptable claim of unattended orchestration.** A worker often finishes precisely after the lead dispatched and stopped. The bridge then waits indefinitely for a human prompt. Worse, `handleStop` intentionally injects only once per continuation chain (`stop_hook_active`), so a new worker result during the follow-up can also wait. For now use a Pi lead when idle wake is required, or an explicitly armed, task-correlated wait/background completion mechanism available in the Claude harness; verify that mechanism in the live harness before promising it. A documented, scoped Herdr wake adapter is a bounded separate experiment if Claude must remain the lead. Do not poll inside hooks or depend on undocumented messaging sockets for production.
- **The final state-write → stdout loss window: defensible residual, not exactly-once delivery.** Budget/ack mismatch and non-atomic owner files are additional, reducible issues and must not be hidden under that label. A next prompt does not prove a prior batch was acted on. State each harness's weaker/stronger consumption witness honestly.
- **Duplicate-over-loss: correct.** The lead must deduplicate by obligation/event identity before side effects. Five failures become dead-lettered, not “delivered eventually”; dead letters need visible attention and explicit recovery. Do not blindly auto-retry every dead letter.
- **Publishing `agentSession`: defensible.** It is required identity evidence, not an ownership credential. Keep tokens out of transcripts and diagnostic exports; retain enough non-secret correlation to debug.

### House style

1. **One trigger, one task, one completion contract.** Frontmatter: name, concise trigger and counter-trigger, harness/prerequisites. No broad “when anything relates to this project” trigger.
2. **Target 40–90 lines and 3–6 kB; hard review threshold 120 lines or 8 kB**, excluding optional reference files but including frontmatter. Also measure the mandatory transitive read cost; target ≤12 kB before the first useful action, with documented exceptions for authoritative tool manuals. Do not meet the cap with thousand-character lines.
3. **Fixed body:** Purpose (2 lines) → Preconditions/authority (≤5 bullets) → Procedure (≤7 numbered steps) → Evidence/stop rules → up to 5 task-triggered reference links. Put critical trust/permission/scope rules before optional links.
4. **No live status, model/version tables, release diary, pane IDs or installed-skill census in a generic skill.** Resolve from code/help/current-state documents. Put task state in the handoff and hard safety gates in executable checks; do not append each postmortem to four skills.
5. **Examples belong beside authoritative code or in references.** Reference links say *when* to load them; do not instruct “read all 501 references.” Keep one writer/source for shared protocol, tiny harness deltas, and explicit precedence for project policy.
6. **Test startup, not just prose.** One fresh-session fixture per harness checks discovery, intended trigger, no accidental auto-dispatch, valid paths/commands, and the first useful action. A cheap link/size check catches `peacock`'s dead documents; a command/schema check catches nonexistent claims. Use source-aware checks, not hardcoded version numbers copied into another file.
7. **Retirement requires evidence.** Keep an opt-in, non-sensitive invocation counter or small manual usage log for a month; count discovery/invocation separately from success. Mtime is only a maintenance signal. Archive and preserve aliases before deletion; no logging prompts, credentials or session text by default.
